## 2. Per-community synthesis

### Hispanic / Latino
- Two independent (a)-type claims say Hispanic consumers over-index on K-beauty as a whole: NIQ 2025 (READ, no number) and CosmeticsDesign 2024 "22% of K-beauty sales growth in 2023" (SNIPPET, page gone). Circana's "36% of TikTok Shop beauty spend" bundles Hispanic with Black households (SNIPPET).
- The only category signal is generic: NIQ 2023 says Hispanics outspend non-Hispanics in facial skin care (all brands, not K-beauty).
- NO press piece ties toner, sunscreen, snail mucin, lip mask, or any other K-beauty category to Latinas. Latin Times has two consumer-service pieces (Target 4-step routine; "Latina glow" vs glass skin), both blocked, neither product-specific in snippets.
- The Belleza Latina survey (n=500, women 18-34) names the Korean glass-skin IDEAL as an inspiration source, which is a routine/aesthetic pull, not a category pull.
- Verdict: strong on "K-beauty overall over-indexes", zero on "which category". Mexico context (61% say K-beauty popular vs ~45% US; export growth) supports demand transfer but is not US data.

### Black / African American
- Richest qualitative record of any community: Highsnobiety, Refinery29, The Root, Essence x2, Blavity, Soko Glam all frame Black women as a distinct K-beauty audience (2021-2025).
- Categories the press ties to Black women, consistently: (1) SUNSCREEN with no white cast (Beauty of Joseon, Innisfree, Neogen, Round Lab), (2) SNAIL MUCIN, (3) hyperpigmentation brighteners (niacinamide, vitamin C, tranexamic acid), (4) sheet masks / gentle essences-toners as the anti-"harsh Western actives" story, (5) double cleansing.
- The defining moment is makeup, not skincare: TIRTIR 9 -> 40 shades after Miss Darcei (Apr 2024), ~56M views, #1 on Amazon beauty (Korea Times, READ). Refinery29 explicitly says Korean SKINCARE already had "broader appeal" with Black consumers before makeup caught up.
- Numbers exist only for Black beauty spend overall ($6.6B 2021; $9.4B McKinsey; $16.2B NIQ 2025) and the shared Circana 36% TikTok Shop figure. No K-beauty-specific Black share found.
- Verdict: journalist + esthetician + creator evidence is consistent and repeated across 6+ outlets; data is absent. Sunscreen and snail mucin are the two categories the press names most.

### Asian American / AAPI
- The one US (a)-type claim naming a driver demographic is NIQ+Spate 2025: "high-income, Asian American Millennials" drive the boom (READ). Nielsen 2025 adds AANHPI women are 83% more likely to have spent >$500 on skincare (READ, skincare overall).
- Older NIQ data (2021, 2024) shows Asian Americans spend 18-23% more on beauty and 34% more on facial/hand/body, with facial moisturizer the standout line ($147/buyer). None of it is K-beauty-specific.
- Counter-evidence on retail floors: Soko Glam ~70% non-Asian customers (2015, brand statement) and Olive Young Pasadena ~50 shoppers with none Korean and fewer than five Asian (2026, reporter count). The Asian base is real online/at Torrance-type stores but is not the visible mainstream buyer.
- Sub-groups: NO US press evidence for Filipino-, Vietnamese-, Chinese-, or Korean-American K-beauty behaviour specifically. Filipino strength is verified only via (i) NIQ Canada and (ii) Philippines-market data (70%+ Korean Wave interest, highest of 28 countries).
- Verdict: Asian American = the highest-spend, highest-income driver segment by NIQ, but never tied to a category; Filipino/South Asian/Chinese over-index is verified for Canada only.

### South Asian
- One (a)-type claim: NIQ Canada 2026 names South Asian households among the three over-indexing groups (READ). Nothing equivalent for the US.
