# Lane A6 — Mechanism layer: skin concerns, physiology, practices and geography by race/ethnicity (US)

Written 2026-09-09. Scope: peer-reviewed / public-health evidence that can explain or refute a community skew per K-beauty basic-skincare category.

Reading discipline: READ = the abstract or page was fetched and read (via NCBI E-utilities, Europe PMC, PMC, publisher page, or an institutional repository). SNIPPET = number seen only in a search-result summary; not verified against the page. "Secondary" = a number read inside a review that cites the primary study. Quotes are verbatim, at most 40 words. Non-US samples are flagged in the design column.

Budget used: 16 WebSearch calls (the session-wide WebSearch cap of 200 was then exhausted by the team, so 6 planned searches never ran), 46 WebFetch calls of which 9 failed (PubMed HTML cookie wall, Wiley, ResearchGate, JAAD, Springer, Cosmetics & Toiletries, Semantic Scholar 429).

## 2. Mechanism per category

Confidence scale: HIGH = ≥2 independent READ sources, at least one US and one with numbers. MOD = one strong READ source plus consistent physiology. LOW = physiology or marketing only, or contradicted.

**Cleanser (foam / oil-balm / double cleansing).** Predicted over-indexer: Asian American (East/SE). The one US practice datum is decisive in direction: Vietnamese women foam-cleanse 7.2 days/week vs 3.5–4.3 for Black, Latina and White women (F37). Physiology agrees: Asian acne is inflammatory-dominant and sebum tracks severity (F04, F05), and East Asian barrier is measured as weak (F14), which is the rationale for low-pH/oil-first double cleansing rather than surfactant scrubbing. Black women cleanse least often (F37) and have low ceramides (F14): if oil/balm cleansers reach them, it is on a dryness story, not an acne story. Confidence: MOD-HIGH for Asian American; LOW for the oil/balm sub-form because no survey splits cleanser type.

**Toner.** Predicted over-indexer: the practice data say Black women (2.2 d/wk vs 1.7 White, 1.4 Vietnamese, 0.9 Latina; F39), which contradicts the marketing intuition that toner is an Asian-routine step. Mechanism for alcohol content: Hispanics self-report the LOWEST alcohol reactivity (F13) and Asians the highest reactivity to temperature/spicy food/itching (F13) with greater measured acute irritant response (F18) and a cultural tendency to report stinging (F17). So the Korean hydrating, alcohol-free toner fits Asian American sensitivity narratives, while the legacy astringent toner habit sits with Black women. Confidence: LOW-MOD (single 70-person Black subsample).

**Toner pad (exfoliating AHA/BHA/PHA pads).** Predicted over-indexer: Asian American and White. The pigment-response mechanism cuts against Black and Hispanic use: irritation from actives is a documented PIH trigger (F08), hyperpigmented macules are already present in 65% of Black and 48% of Hispanic women vs 18% Asian (F03), so an exfoliating pad has the highest downside where PIH baseline is highest. No survey measures pad use. Confidence: LOW (physiology only; note that gentle PHA pads marketed as PIH-safe could flip this).

**Essence.** Predicted over-indexer: Asian American. No practice or physiology evidence specific to the form. Indirect: East Asian barrier "low maturation, relatively weak" (F14), dry/dull skin ranks among the frequently bothersome concerns for US Asian women (F26), and Asian American skin-care spend indexes 170 (F44). Confidence: LOW.

**Serum / ampoule, by hero ingredient.**
- Niacinamide / snail mucin / cica / rice (brightening, PIH, barrier): the strongest mechanism in this lane. Hyperpigmentation is the dominant excess concern in Black and Hispanic women (F03, F06, F07, F01), the #1 concern of US East/SE Asian women and tied #3 for Indian/Central-SW Asian women (F26), and "uneven tone" 21% + "dark spots" 18% among South Asian Americans (F24). Predicted over-indexers: Black, Hispanic, South Asian; East/SE Asian on the concern side. Caveat: purchase lags need for Black and Latina women, whose face-cream use is the lowest (F38). Confidence: HIGH on the need, MOD on the purchase.
- Retinol: predicted over-indexer White. Wrinkle onset is a decade later in people of color (F28), the broader (non-Asian) female population is the group most concerned with upper-facial wrinkles (F26), and retinoid dermatitis (50% in a cited series) converts to PIH in darker skin (F08), which is why Korean low-strength or encapsulated retinol positioning matters for skin of color. Confidence: MOD.
- Propolis: no evidence found. Cica for sensitivity: sensitive-skin self-report is 52% with no ethnic difference (F12), so "for sensitive skin" claims do not ethnically skew; only Asian reactivity reports (F13, F17, F18, F19) lean it Asian American. Confidence: LOW.

**Moisturizer / cream.** Split by site. Body moisturizer: Black and Latina women 6.0 d/wk vs White 4.8, Vietnamese 4.5 (F40); Black lotion dollar share exceeds household share (F45); low ceramides in Black stratum corneum (F14); AD odds 1.70–2.71x in Black children (F21, F22) and 28.7% self-reported eczema in elderly Black adults (F23); dry skin is the #1 concern of South Asian Americans (F24). Face cream: White women highest (4.1) and Latina lowest (2.4) (F38). Predicted over-indexers: Black, Hispanic, South Asian for rich body/barrier creams; White and East Asian for face creams. Climate cuts against the Black skew: Black concentration is in the humid South and Mid-Atlantic (F51) where humidity lowers eczema odds (F53), whereas the dry-cold barrier stress (F54) sits with the Northeast/Midwest and the arid Southwest (Hispanic: NM 47.7%, AZ 30.7%, NV 28.7%; F50). Confidence: MOD.

**Sunscreen.** Practice: NH White 40.4%, Hispanic 24.7%, NH Black 10.9% use SPF15+ consistently (F29); Asian American 29% vs 35% NHW (F33); Hispanic use rising fastest, 31.1% to 44.6% (F34) and reaching 32.3% among sun-sensitive Hispanic adults, higher with English-language acculturation (F31). Barriers are exactly what Korean chemical-filter, no-white-cast formulas remove: white cast 25% SOC vs 7% NHW (F32), greasiness cited by over half of Asian and one-third of Hispanic non-users (F30). Mechanism: visible light pigments dark skin and drives melasma/PIH (F35, F36), and melasma is 8.8% of premenopausal Latinas (F9) with up to 36% in Latino migrant men (F10). Predicted over-indexer for the Korean sunscreen form: Asian American (highest baseline willingness plus the greasiness complaint), then Hispanic (fastest-growing use, melasma need, Sun Belt geography: F50). Black consumers have the largest unmet need but the lowest habit; note that for visible-light protection a sheer chemical filter is NOT the mechanistic answer, a tinted iron-oxide filter is (F35). Confidence: MOD-HIGH.

**Sheet mask.** Refuted skew: face-mask use did not differ by race/ethnicity in the only US practice survey (F42). Weak Asian American lean from dry/dull-skin concern (F26) and spend index (F44). Confidence: LOW; treat any strong ethnic claim for masks as marketing.

**Spot / pimple patch.** Predicted over-indexer: Asian American first, Black and Hispanic mechanistically. Acne prevalence: Black 37%, Hispanic 32%, Asian 30%, White 24%, Indian 23% (F02); Asian acne is inflammatory-dominant (F04), the lesion type hydrocolloid patches address; the patch's no-pick benefit is worth most where acne-induced PIH is 65.3% (Black) and 52.7% (Hispanic) (F06). South Asian Americans: acne is the #1 diagnosis at 37% (F24). Confidence: MOD.

**Lip mask.** No ethnic mechanism. Lip-balm use is near-uniform (Vietnamese 5.0, White 5.0, Black 4.7, Latina 4.0; F41). Only a climate lean: cold, dry air is the documented barrier stressor (F54), which favors the Northeast/Midwest/Pacific Northwest, i.e., Asian (NJ 10.2%, NY 9.6%, WA 9.5%; F52) and White geography. Confidence: LOW.

**Face mist.** No literature at all for the form. Only a climate argument: low relative humidity raises barrier stress (F53, F54) and the driest states are the Hispanic core (NM, AZ, NV; F50). Confidence: VERY LOW.

**Eye cream (dark circles).** Predicted over-indexer: South Asian first, East/SE Asian second. Dark circles are the #1 facial concern of Indian/Central-SW Asian US women and #2 for all Asian women (F26); the Indian clinic series shows dark circles are mostly constitutional (51.5%) and familial (63% family history) with onset at 16–25 (F27), which means demand is chronic and treatment response is limited, i.e., a repeat-purchase category with high dissatisfaction. Under-eye bags rank #3 for East/SE Asian women (F26), a different (structural) need. Confidence: MOD (one US survey, one non-US clinic series, no US prevalence).

Cross-cutting: "sensitive skin" does not skew by ethnicity (F12), "photoaging/wrinkle" skews White (F26, F28), "hyperpigmentation" skews Black > Hispanic > South Asian ≈ East Asian (F03, F24, F26), and every skin-of-color category carries the PIH-from-irritation constraint (F08) that favors the gentle Korean formulation philosophy over Western high-strength actives.

## 4. Ten strongest findings, ranked

1. F03 — Hyperpigmented macules: Black 65%, Hispanic 48%, White 25%, Asian 18%, Indian 10% (N=2895 women, JEADV 2011). The single best mechanism for brightening serums.
2. F29 — Consistent SPF15+ use: NH Black 10.9%, Hispanic 24.7%, NH White 40.4% (NHIS 2015). Sunscreen skews White by habit, not by need.
3. F37 — Foaming facial cleanser 7.2 d/wk for Vietnamese women vs 3.5–4.3 for Black, Latina, White (Taking Stock, CA, N=318). Cleansing is the most Asian-skewed practice measured.
4. F32 — White-cast concern 25% of skin-of-color vs 7% NHW; reapplication 45% vs 76% (JDD 2024). The exact barrier a no-cast Korean filter removes.
5. F02/F04 — Acne: Black 37%, Hispanic 32%, Asian 30%, White 24%; Asian acne inflammatory 20% vs comedonal 10% (JEADV 2011). Patches fit Asian lesion type.
6. F26 — US Asian women: hyperpigmentation #1 (East/SE Asian), dark circles #1 (Indian/Central-SW Asian), wrinkles for everyone else (MOSAIC, N=652 of 3,974).
7. F40/F38 — Body lotion 6.0 d/wk Black and Latina vs 4.8 White, 4.5 Vietnamese; face cream White 4.1 vs Latina 2.4 (Taking Stock). Moisturizer splits body vs face by group.
8. F14 — Black stratum corneum: low ceramides, high cohesion; East Asian: weak barrier, low maturation (J Dermatol Sci 2010). Physiology behind dryness (Black) and sensitivity (Asian).
9. F22 — Atopic dermatitis: NH Black children aOR 2.71 incidence; persistence aOR 6.26 Black, 6.42 Hispanic (Project Viva, N=1,437).
10. F09/F24 — Melasma 8.8% of 500 premenopausal Latinas in Dallas–Fort Worth; dry skin is the #1 stated concern (25%) of South Asian Americans (N=190).
